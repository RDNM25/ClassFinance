## Design Specificity Verdict

Class Finance feels grounded in its Indonesian labels, rupiah amounts, and treasurer workflows. Its red sidebar, rounded cards, and controls still resemble a general admin dashboard; stronger cues around traceable money movement could make the product feel more purpose-built.

The deterministic scan returned [] (zero findings), but it does not provide meaningful coverage for native WPF/XAML. I visually inspected the login screen; the authenticated pages were reviewed from source. The browser preview and overlay are not applicable to this desktop app.

## Overall Impression

The login screen has a clear hierarchy: labeled username and password fields, an eye control, and a prominent sign-in button. Inside the app, the workflows cover practical finance tasks, but Tagihan presents too much detail at once. The biggest opportunity is making totals, filtered results, and consequential payment actions easier to interpret together.

## What’s Working

- The four labeled navigation items make the main sections easy to recognize.
- Indonesian terminology, rupiah formatting, and visible payment statuses suit the treasurer’s daily tasks.
- Student search, date filtering, input validation, and delete confirmations provide useful controls.

## Priority Issues

1. **[P1] History totals can contradict the transaction list filter.** In RefreshTransaksi, income and expense totals follow the date filter, while the Kas Masuk/Kas Keluar selector only filters the rows; Total Kas remains the current balance. This can leave users comparing an incoming-only list with totals that still include outgoing activity. Make each summary’s scope explicit and keep the type filter behavior consistent. Suggested command: $impeccable clarify. Source: ClassFinance/Views/RiwayatTransaksiPage.xaml.cs:170.

2. **[P1] Tagihan is dense, especially when the window is narrow.** The page stacks the bill list and payment details into equal-height rows below 840 px, while the detail area includes student balances, statuses, nested transactions, and multiple payment actions. Keep the student summary compact, reveal transaction history on demand, and emphasize the next appropriate action. Suggested command: $impeccable layout. Source: ClassFinance/Views/TagihanPage.xaml.cs:67.

3. **[P2] “Data Siswa” silently omits students with no unpaid bills.** The dashboard list filters out students whose bills are all paid, so its title sounds like a full roster when it is actually a follow-up list. Rename it to reflect that subset or provide a way to see every student. Suggested command: $impeccable clarify. Source: ClassFinance/Views/DashboardPage.xaml.cs:93.

4. **[P2] Edit and delete actions are hard to discover and identify.** Transaction and bill rows use ••• buttons without a visible action label or descriptive accessibility name. Name them “Aksi” or “More actions” for assistive technology, and add a short visible cue where space allows. Suggested command: $impeccable audit. Sources: ClassFinance/Views/TagihanPage.xaml:115 and ClassFinance/Views/RiwayatTransaksiPage.xaml:187.

5. **[P2] Consequential payment actions look too similar.** “Tandai refund lunas,” “Tandai Lunas,” and “Bayar Manual” share the same outline-button treatment. Separate routine payment from refund completion and clarify the effect before the user confirms it. Suggested command: $impeccable polish. Source: ClassFinance/Views/TagihanPage.xaml:231.

## Cognitive Load

The Tagihan detail area has high cognitive load: student summaries, nested transaction history, and multiple payment actions compete for attention. The two-panel grouping helps, but chunking, action hierarchy, and progressive disclosure need improvement.

## Persona Red Flags

- **Alex, power-user treasurer:** Filtering to Kas Masuk still leaves expense totals visible, adding extra reconciliation.
- **Jordan, first-time treasurer:** Data Siswa omits fully paid students without explaining that it is a follow-up list; no in-app help is visible.
- **Sam, accessibility-dependent user:** The ••• controls don’t expose the available actions through a descriptive name.

## Minor Observations

- The login card’s large red header is visually dominant, though the fields and primary action remain clear.
- Shared theme brushes and fixed colors coexist; verify status and selection colors in both modes.
- Empty states could point users toward the next useful action.

## Questions to Consider

- When Kas Masuk is active, should the summary cards recalculate to match that filtered list, or always show all activity for the selected date?
- Should the Dashboard show every student or intentionally show only students who need follow-up?
